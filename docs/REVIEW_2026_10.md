# Opus review and follow-up work

On 6 October 2026, the requested **Opus 5.5** reviewed an immutable copy of public source commit `f1bc97059ff2e3492a819b1a3897cba82c2aaf32`. The authenticated CLI reported `claude-opus-5-5`; no substitute model was used. Its tools could read source, but could not edit it, deploy, operate a browser or inspect member data.

The first review covered the encrypted client, identity and recovery, import/export, sharing, hosting and release claims. A separate review covered the older application, federation, hosting tools and remaining public interfaces without receiving the first review's findings. Together they reported **41 findings: no critical findings, two high-priority findings, 15 medium-priority findings and 24 lower-priority findings**. These are the reviewers' classifications, not a certification or a count of independently demonstrated exploits. Some findings describe limitations already disclosed by the project.

Follow-up reviews examined the fixes against separate immutable snapshots. One reported nine new or residual issues; a later delta review identified four narrower issues in recovery validation, expired sessions, import-error classification and older photo limits. Reproduced issues are fixed and tested; an SDK behavior described as an inference was checked against the installed dependency before changing it. Resource limits and refusal to trust a changed friend identity remain deliberate safety boundaries, with their compatibility costs disclosed. A final narrow review accepted the recovery-dialog, departed-history and mobile-layout fixes without blocking findings. Its lower-priority concurrency, stale-session and cancellation notes received bounded follow-up fixes. Failure to retrieve departed history still stops the download with an error rather than claiming a complete export.

The reviews found the encryption foundation credible but did **not** recommend personal-data production use. Source inspection cannot establish browser behavior, deployment integrity, dependency safety or usability. The private review transcripts are not published: they are operational records, not a community support channel.

## What changed

- **A damaged saved part no longer closes the whole book.** Available memories and account controls remain usable; individual downloads identify unavailable content.
- **Recovery can resume after interruption.** Retrying preserves the existing signing identity and recovery key. A failed sign-in retains the exact session for retry. An already-ended server session can still clear its local keys; a separate warned choice can remove this browser’s keys while its home is unreachable. A second tab cannot erase the first tab's active device.
- **A device check cannot silently become account recovery.** Same-account verification requests are refused. A new browser uses the saved recovery kit; friend checks wait for authenticated SDK trust before enabling sharing. Rate-limited verification sends preserve authentication-message ordering.
- **Imports handle ordinary large collections.** Albums refer to their photos without copying the whole album into one record. Long message histories are split without truncation. Missing and unsupported items have visible warnings. Nested legacy exports use encrypted temporary browser storage and remove it after completion or cancellation.
- **Sharing includes photos and durable retries.** The browser checks image dimensions before decoding, removes metadata from shared copies and retains an interrupted post's exact content and selected audience. Replies, reactions and posts keep stable operation identities when retried.
- **Reading and leaving are practical.** Feed pages load photos as needed. New imports have a browser-encrypted text index. Individual conversations, including available history after a friendship ends, can be downloaded in complete parts. Download progress survives background updates and page navigation. The export explicitly includes earlier copies marked removed.
- **Controls remain understandable.** Draft text, selected photos and audience survive refreshes. New posts announce an available update instead of replacing a draft. Friend checks use a keyboard-contained dialog; navigation and errors expose their state to assistive tools.
- **Host maintenance has clearer evidence.** Backups stage on the data disk, use a fenced final copy, and record local health. Retention requires an explicit preview and application. Container limits and a local health timer are included.
- **The older application keeps its privacy boundaries during cleanup.** Imported photos cannot disappear while another import is using them. Remote revocation removes content after its last local reader loses access. New federation requests have a bounded delivery opportunity even when old removals are waiting.

Behavioral tests include real Synapse servers, the installed Matrix SDK and actual browser encryption. The [client qualification](../encrypted-client/QUALIFICATION.md) and [host qualification](../encrypted-host/QUALIFICATION.md) distinguish passing runs, failed attempts, capacity measurements and untested environments.

## What still prevents personal-data production use

**The current host-image scans contain unresolved security advisories.** Updated official PostgreSQL and Caddy images reduce the reported findings, but do not clear them. Synapse and restic also have findings. A reproducible, project-owned restic security-layer experiment has separate scan and recovery evidence; it is not silently substituted for the pinned official runtime. [Exact scope and maintenance procedure](ENCRYPTED_DEPENDENCY_UPGRADES.md).

Independent human security and accessibility reviews and the five-person usability study remain open. Neither an AI review nor passing tests completes them. The browser publisher remains trusted, recipients can save shared copies, and hosts see account and connection metadata. Keep using fictional data and retain original archives while these checks are unfinished.
