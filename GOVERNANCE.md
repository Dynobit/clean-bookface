# A project that can outlast its founder

Clean Bookface should belong to the people who keep it useful. Nobody should have to trust a founder forever just to keep their own memories.

**Current state:** an owner-maintained v0.1 public-preview project under the Dynobit GitHub account, not an established community foundation. The owner has approved source and project-website publication. No community maintainers, fiscal sponsor or successor organization have been appointed by this document. Transfer of accounts remains an explicit owner decision. The commitments below guide that transition.

## What contributors are looking after

Keep imports private, make sharing deliberate, preserve a chronological feed, and make export and deletion work. The official project has no advertising, tracking or bot personas. Accessibility software, build tools and automated tests are welcome; the ban concerns accounts pretending to participate as people.

The source is MIT licensed. These commitments describe the official project's direction; the MIT license does not prohibit commercial forks or guarantee that every fork follows our policies. Credit contributors accurately. Use chosen public names without requiring legal names in public contribution records.

## From founder to maintainers

1. Publish reviewed releases under the current Dynobit namespace. A later transfer to a dedicated project organization requires the owner’s agreement and consenting maintainers ready to take responsibility. Review account/profile links and Git history before claiming public pseudonymity.
2. Invite contributions through small, reproducible issues and useful documentation work. Earn responsibility through sustained, reviewed contributions, including maintenance and support.
3. Record consenting maintainers by their chosen handles, scopes and appointment dates in a public maintainer list. Add at least two trusted human organization owners before the founder steps back. Use individual accounts, two-factor authentication and documented recovery; never a shared public password.
4. Publish proposals for adding or removing maintainers. Give existing maintainers time to respond and record the reasons and decision. Once there is a maintainer group, aim for agreement; unresolved ordinary decisions use a recorded majority of active maintainers, with conflicts disclosed. A tie keeps the current behavior while alternatives are discussed.
5. Give every consequential change an independent review once a second qualified maintainer is available. Security fixes can remain private until users have a safe update. Record emergency changes afterward without disclosing private reports or user data.

Before that group exists, the repository owner remains responsible. An unfilled role is an unfilled role, not evidence that a community is maintaining the service.

## Transfer the responsibilities, not just the repository

Maintain a private handover checklist for the domain, DNS, repository, release permissions, project website, funding, security contact and recovery material. Record who renews each service and a backup contact. Keep personal billing details and recovery secrets out of source control.

Before a founder departure, the incoming maintainers should independently build a release, update the project website and prove account recovery. Transfer ownership through each provider's supported process, verify the new owners' access, then remove obsolete access. A domain's registrant, a GitHub organization owner and a fiscal sponsor are different roles; a repository transfer does not transfer all three.

A fiscal host such as [Open Source Collective](https://opencollective.com/opensource/apply/intro) may later help with donations and expenses, subject to its application, verification and fees. It does not automatically provide maintainers or accept ownership of every project asset. Agree domain and other asset custody explicitly.

## Software maintainers are not every circle's administrators

Community access to source does not grant access to members' archives, production databases, signing keys or backups. Each independent circle chooses its host, explains that administrator's access, and handles local moderation and recovery. Public bug reports contain only synthetic examples.

The project website should explain the software, publish documentation and point to releases. An optional demonstration must use fictional content. Hosting a public account service would add separate operating, moderation, funding and privacy responsibilities; this document does not appoint anyone to do that.

## An honest exit

If maintainers cannot continue, publish a maintenance notice and a call for successors. Preserve source, documented export paths and a clear release archive. Do not quietly abandon a service containing people's memories or give strangers its production credentials. A successor is chosen and handed verified responsibilities; open source makes that possible, not automatic.
